Bagasse vs Plastic: Which Is Right for Your Food Business?

28 September 2026 · 4 min read · By the SLD team

Bagasse tableware is made from sugarcane fibre; plastic disposables are made from polymers such as polypropylene or polystyrene. Both have a place. The useful question is not which is better, but which is better for a particular job.

Heat and oil

Bagasse handles hot, oily food comfortably and is microwave-safe. Thin plastic plates can warp with very hot food, while good PP containers handle heat well and can be reheated.

Liquids

For plated food and thick gravies, bagasse performs well. For thin liquids carried over distance, a PP container with a click-fit lid is more secure.

Compliance

India's single-use plastic rules prohibit items such as plastic plates, cups, cutlery and polystyrene (thermocol) tableware. Bagasse plates and bowls are an accepted replacement. PP containers with lids are a different product class and remain widely used, though it is wise to check local rules.

Cost

Per piece, bagasse usually costs more than the plastic items it replaces. Bought by the carton, the difference narrows — and many restaurants find customers respond well to compostable packaging.

A sensible split

  • Plates, bowls, trays and clamshells: bagasse.
  • Thin gravies, dals and chutneys: PP containers.
  • Biryani and baked dishes: aluminium foil.
